## Fact checks (web search, 2026-11-22)

- **Coelacanth (A):** first living specimen caught 22–23 Dec 1938 off the
  Chalumna River, South Africa; museum curator Marjorie Courtenay-Latimer
  (East London Museum) sketched it and sent the drawing to ichthyologist
  J. L. B. Smith, who recognised it as a coelacanth — a lineage thought
  extinct for ~66 million years. Three-lobed tail and lobe fins confirmed
  (Guinness World Records, Ocean Conservancy, National Geographic).
- **Wood frog (B):** freezes solid in winter, up to 65–70% of body water as
  ice; heart stops, no detectable heartbeat or brain activity; thaws in
  spring and resumes normal activity within ~48 hours (Cal Academy,
  National Geographic, Univ. of Colorado).
- **Axolotl (C):** neotenic — keeps larval gills and never metamorphoses;
  regenerates limbs, tail, spinal cord, heart tissue and parts of the brain
  without scarring (Cell, Aeon, Wiley).
- **Sunlight (D):** light takes ~8 minutes to travel from the Sun to Earth
  (standard astronomy fact; also the device of awe #20).
